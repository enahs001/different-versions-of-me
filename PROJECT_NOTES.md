# Project notes: The Different Versions of Me

## Artistic concept

The site is a self-portrait you move through rather than read. Each role I hold (student, president, intern, tutor, friend) is a room with a surface and an underside. Visitors have to *act* to see past the performance: drag a seam to peel back the polished version, turn a mask over to find a mirror. The effort is the point. People usually only see the surface of a role unless they choose to look further.

Typography carries the theme. Upright serif is the public voice: certain, composed, expected. Italic is the private voice: honest, uneven, still forming. The two voices appear on every page.

"Just Me" breaks the pattern on purpose. It's the only light page, it has no version number, its headline is entirely in the private voice, and it is built around unresolved things: contradictions on sliders with no right answer, sentences I've crossed out and rewritten, and a closing line with a blinking cursor.

The words a visitor "carries" out of one role arrive with them in the next, so the roles are linked by what moves between them. The ending removes the titles letter by letter until only the values remain.

## Connection to course themes

| Theme | Where it shows up |
| --- | --- |
| Professional identity and performance | The polished surface of each role; the mask's script |
| Expectations and authenticity | Mask vs. mirror on every page; the finale removes titles and expectations together |
| Internal parts and contradictions | "What you don't see"; the contradictions sliders on Just Me; crossed-out drafts |
| Mutual aid and reciprocity | Connections (e.g. Tutor + Friend: generosity without keeping score); "Opportunities should be passed on" |
| Relationality and right relationship | The connections web: identity as relationships between parts, not separate labels; carried qualities |
| Taking sides / acting on values | "Where I take a side" on Just Me; the final statement about how I choose to show up |

## Questions for user testing

1. Without instructions, did you find out that the surface could be dragged? How long did it take?
2. After flipping the mask, what did you feel or think? Did any mirror line feel familiar from your own life?
3. Which version of me did you expect to know best? Did the site change that?
4. Did "Just Me" feel different from the other pages? In what way?
5. In the connections map, which pair surprised you, and which felt obvious?
6. When the titles disappeared, what did you notice first?
7. Was anything confusing, too slow, or too fast? (Ask one person to try with reduced motion or keyboard only.)
8. In one sentence: what is this site about?
